# Visual art direction: D2-dark gothic body-horror, hand-painted sprites, vivid VFX

The visual identity leans hard into D2's side of the cross, not Lost Ark's. **Tonal range**: muted gothic backgrounds (mossy stone, dim torchlight, fog, dusk light) overlaid with brilliantly vivid combat VFX — the contrast is the whole identity. A fireball *detonates* because the world around it is desaturated. **Sprite style**: hand-painted illustrated sprites, not pixel art — pixel art codes "indie/retro" while we're targeting "real MMO." **Monster aesthetic**: gothic-medieval with body-horror accents (undead, demons, corrupted knights, blood priests, fungal infestations, parasitic insects). Stylized gore (blood spurts, body-on-ground decay), never edgelord splatter. **Lighting**: fixed per zone, no global day/night cycle — zone mood comes from zone-specific lighting (Ashen Plains is perpetually dusk; Frozen Cathedral is perpetually twilight). **Palette discipline**: each zone owns a ~32-color local palette but UI accent colors are *constant across the entire game* (HP red, Spirit blue, Wrath orange, rarity colors matching D2's exact convention: white → blue → yellow → green → gold). **VFX gets its own design pass** — spells are 60% of how combat feels and need ~2 weeks of dedicated VFX work per discipline at launch. **Art budget assumption**: $3–10k of paid freelance/stock art over the first 18 months; stock packs (CraftPix, OpenGameArt, Itch) cover environments and UI, commissioned art for signature monsters/bosses/zones/items. The deliberate departure from Lost Ark vivid-everywhere is contrarian and ownable — saturated cheerful ARPG is now a saturated genre. Going dark trades some Steam-thumbnail attraction for game identity, which is the right trade for a project where identity matters more than discovery-funnel.
